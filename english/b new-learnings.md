# Long Time Sentences
## It’s been a long time since
It means: A lot of time has passed from that event until now.
- It’s been a long time since we met.
- - We met a long time ago, and now we are talking about it.
- It’s been a long time since you last messaged me.
- - Your last message was a long time ago, and you haven't messaged me since.
- It’s been 2 hours since I last saw you.
- - I saw you 2 hours ago, and 2 hours have passed until now.

Note: You can replace “a long time” with an exact or approximate duration:
- It’s been two days since I called you.
- It’s been three months since I visited Peshawar.
- It’s been years since we last talked.
- It’s been a while since I saw you.

## After a long time
This one is about doing something again after a long gap.
After a long time, I ate biryani.
This means you hadn't eaten biryani for a long time, and then you ate it.
But in natural everyday English, I'd usually say:
I had biryani for the first time in a long time.
or
I finally had biryani again after a long time.
For example:
- I am having biryani after a long time.
- I finally went to the gym again after a long time.
- I watched a movie after a long time.
- I played football again after a long time.

# One situation, many ways
Imagine you haven't seen your friend for one year.
You meet him today.
You could say:
- Long time no see!
- Wow, it's been so long!
- It's been a long time since we last met.
- It's been ages since I saw you.
- We haven't seen each other in a year.
- It's been a year since we last met.
All are talking about the same basic situation. But each sentence has a different **style**, **focus**, and **feeling**.
